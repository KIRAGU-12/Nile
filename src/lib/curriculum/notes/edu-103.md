## Unit overview

Introduction to Education and Teaching Practice orients first-year students to the Kenyan education system, the professional identity of the teacher, and the basic principles of effective classroom practice. The unit situates the B.Ed (Arts) within the national 2-6-3-4 system and the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), emphasising the teacher's role as facilitator, mentor and assessor rather than mere transmitter of knowledge. For Kenyan students, the unit answers the question "What does it mean to be a teacher?" by linking policy frameworks to daily classroom decisions about lesson planning, inclusive practice and assessment.

The unit also introduces foundational concepts in curriculum studies and educational psychology, preparing students for the more specialised pedagogy units they will encounter in later semesters. Understanding lesson planning, learning outcomes and assessment methods is essential for the Teaching Practice component that runs throughout the degree. For students who will go on to teach English and literature in Kenyan secondary schools, the unit connects the content knowledge of their Arts degree to the practical demands of the classroom.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, students should be able to:
- Outline the structure of the Kenyan education system and explain the principles of the Competency-Based Curriculum.
- Describe the professional role and ethical responsibilities of the Kenyan teacher.
- Apply basic principles of lesson planning, including objectives, activities, resources and assessment.
- Distinguish between formative and summative assessment and design simple assessment tools.
- Explain the importance of inclusive education and differentiated instruction in Kenyan classrooms.
- Reflect critically on their own emerging teacher identity and professional values.

## Detailed notes

### The Kenyan education system and the teacher's role

Kenya's education structure is currently organised as 2-6-3-4: two years of pre-primary, six years of primary, three years of junior secondary, three years of senior secondary and a minimum of four years of university. The Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), introduced in 2017, replaces the former 8-4-4 system and emphasises **core competencies** (communication and collaboration, critical thinking and problem solving, creativity and imagination, citizenship, digital literacy, learning to learn, self-efficacy) rather than rote content mastery. The teacher in the CBC model acts as a **facilitator** and **assessor**, guiding learners through activities, projects and collaborative tasks. Professionalism, subject knowledge, communication skills and ethical conduct are central to this role. Teachers must also understand curriculum aims and learners' diverse needs, including inclusive practice for learners with disabilities and those from marginalised communities.

### Lesson planning and teaching strategies

A lesson plan states the **objective**, the **learning outcomes**, the **teaching and learning activities**, the **resources** required and how learning will be **assessed**. Effective objectives are SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Teaching strategies include teacher-centred methods (lecture, demonstration) and learner-centred methods (group work, inquiry, problem-solving). In the teaching of English, learner-centred approaches include drama activities, debate, poetry recitation, reading circles and creative writing workshops. Differentiation — adjusting content, process or product to meet diverse learners — is a professional expectation under the CBC.

### Assessment: formative and summative

Assessment can be **formative** (during learning: questioning, peer feedback, drafts, quizzes) or **summative** (end of unit, term or year: examinations, standardised tests). Valid assessment measures intended outcomes fairly and reliably; it should be aligned with learning objectives and transparent to learners. In Kenyan schools, formative assessment includes class tests, oral presentations and homework; summative assessment includes the Kenya Certificate of Primary Education (KCPE) and the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE). Teachers are expected to record and report learner progress systematically.

### Inclusive education and classroom management

Inclusive education means ensuring that all learners, regardless of ability, background or circumstance, can participate fully in classroom life. Strategies include differentiated instruction, use of local languages as bridges to English, accessible learning materials and positive behavioural support. Classroom management — establishing routines, expectations and a safe environment — is the precondition for effective teaching. Professional ethics require teachers to maintain confidentiality, avoid corporal punishment and respect learners' dignity.

## Practice exercises (exam revision)

**Short answer**
1. List the four core competencies of the Kenyan CBC and explain how one of them relates to the teaching of literature. (Easy)
2. Define "formative assessment" and give two examples used in an English classroom. (Easy)
3. What is the difference between a teaching objective and a learning outcome? (Medium)

**Essay questions**
1. "The teacher in the CBC is a facilitator rather than a transmitter of knowledge." Discuss with reference to classroom practice in Kenyan schools. (Hard)
2. Analyse the strengths and weaknesses of lesson planning as a tool for improving teaching quality. (Medium)
3. How can formative assessment improve learner outcomes in English and literature? (Medium)

**Objective / passage-based**
1. Read the following lesson plan extract and answer the questions that follow. (Medium)
   Grade: Form Two | Subject: English | Duration: 40 minutes
   Topic: Elements of Poetry
   Objective: By the end of the lesson, learners will be able to identify and explain three poetic devices in a given poem.
   Activities: Teacher models identification of metaphor and simile; learners work in pairs to annotate a poem; whole-class feedback.
   Assessment: Observation during group work; collection of annotated poems.
   a. Is the objective SMART? Justify your answer. (3 marks)
   b. Identify one learner-centred strategy and one teacher-centred strategy. (2 marks)
   c. Suggest one formative assessment task not already included. (2 marks)
